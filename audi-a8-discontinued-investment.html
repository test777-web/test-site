The Audi A8 Is Done After 2026: Should You Buy One While You Still Can?

By AutoGlob Staff · July 24, 2026 · 6 min read

After the 2026 model year, Audi is retiring the A8. The brand's flagship sedan, the car that introduced the aluminum spaceframe, the car that's carried Audi's most advanced tech to market first for three decades, is stepping aside for the Q9, a three row SUV that will become the new range topper.

It's not a surprising move. It's a predictable one, and that's almost worse. Sedan sales have been sliding across the segment for years, and flagship sedans specifically have become halo products that automakers keep around for prestige rather than volume. Mercedes has hinted at similar questions about the S Class's long term future. Audi just moved first.

The irony: it's going out with a real incentive

Right now, dealers are offering $10,000 in consumer cash on the outgoing A8, on top of another $10,000 in dealer cash that isn't advertised but can often be negotiated. On a car starting just over $96,000, that's more than 20 percent off sticker for a flagship sedan that, six months ago, would have carried no discount at all.

The last A8s are being sold like a product nobody wants, at the exact moment they became the most complete version of themselves.

Does last of its kind mean smart buy

Here's the tension. Final model year cars sometimes become genuinely collectible, the last of a lineage, built at peak refinement, before the platform disappears. But that pattern mostly applies to enthusiast cars: manual sports coupes, V8 muscle cars, anything with a fan base willing to pay a premium for scarcity years later.

A large luxury sedan aimed at chauffeured executives and long distance comfort doesn't have that kind of fan base. Depreciation on outgoing flagship sedans tends to be steep and fast, not slow and appreciating. Buyers who want a flagship sedan gravitate toward whatever's newest, and a discontinued nameplate loses resale leverage the moment the successor is announced.

Where this actually makes sense

If you're buying an A8 to drive it, genuinely use it as a car, keep it five plus years, enjoy the discount, the math works today better than it has in a decade. Twenty percent off a flagship sedan with three decades of engineering behind it is a real deal for someone who wants the car, not the badge trajectory.

If you're buying it hoping the last A8 becomes a future classic the way a last of run manual coupe might, the collector case is much weaker. Large luxury sedans depreciate on a different curve than enthusiast cars, and discontinued doesn't automatically mean desirable ten years down the line.

The verdict

Buy the last A8 because it's a genuinely well sorted flagship at a real discount, not because you're betting on it becoming valuable. The Q9 is coming either way, and Audi's flagship story moves on with or without you.
