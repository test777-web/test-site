I Drove the 2026 Toyota GR86 Yuzu Edition: Here's My Honest Review

By AutoGlob Staff · July 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Toyota didn't reinvent the GR86 for the Yuzu Edition. It bolted on a limited run citrus yellow paint job, black 18 inch wheels, and a set of yuzu stitched seats, then called it a day. On paper that sounds like a marketing exercise. In practice it's a reminder of something the car industry keeps forgetting: sometimes the car was already good enough.

The GR86 has never been about numbers. Its 2.4 liter flat four makes a modest 228 horsepower, and 0 to 60 still takes a leisurely 6.1 seconds. A base Camry will out drag it without trying. But horsepower was never the point of this car, and the Yuzu Edition doesn't pretend otherwise.

The twist nobody expected

What's ironic is that in a year when every other manufacturer is racing to add software subscriptions and screens, Toyota's special edition strategy was to remove distractions. There's no upgraded infotainment. No extra driver assist suite. Just a genuinely sorted chassis, a six speed manual, and a limited slip differential doing what they've always done: communicating exactly what the rear tires are up to, in real time, without a computer translating it for you.

It's the rare car that feels more expensive to drive than it is to buy.

On a tight two lane road outside the city, the GR86 rotates with a kind of honesty that's disappeared from most of the segment. Trail brake into a corner and the back end steps out predictably, correctable with a small steering input and a squeeze of throttle. Nothing about it feels artificial. Nothing about it feels like it's protecting you from your own mistakes.

Living with it day to day

The cabin is tight, the trunk is small, and the ride is firm enough that you'll notice every expansion joint on the highway. None of that matters much if the car spends most weekends being driven for the sake of driving. It matters a great deal if it's your only car and you have a commute involving potholes and passengers.

Fuel economy lands around 25 mpg combined, unremarkable, but not the point of the exercise. Toyota's reliability reputation carries over, and the GR86 platform has now been out long enough that most of its early ownership quirks are well documented online.

The verdict

The Yuzu Edition doesn't change what the GR86 is. It just repaints a genuinely great small sports car and asks buyers to pay a modest premium for the privilege of standing out at a Cars and Coffee meet. If you were already sold on the GR86's formula, light weight over raw power, feedback over isolation, this is a fine reason to buy the anniversary adjacent color. If you weren't sold already, the yellow paint won't change your mind, and it shouldn't have to.
